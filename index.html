Altitude safety

The higher you go, the less oxygen each breath gives you. Know which zone you are in.

Low

Below 1,500 m

Altitude problems are very rare.

Moderate

1,500 – 2,500 m

Mild effects possible for sensitive people.

High

2,500 – 3,500 m

Altitude sickness can begin. Ascend slowly.

Very high

3,500 – 5,500 m

Common sickness. Acclimatise and rest often.

Extreme

Above 5,500 m

Body cannot fully adapt. Experts only.

Symptoms

  • Headache that does not go away
  • Nausea or loss of appetite
  • Dizziness and unusual tiredness
  • Trouble sleeping
  • Severe cases: confusion, breathlessness at rest, a wet cough

Prevention

  • Climb slowly. Above 3,000 m, add no more than 500 m of sleeping height per day.
  • Take a rest day every 3 to 4 days.
  • Drink 3 to 4 litres of water daily.
  • Avoid alcohol and smoking.
  • Climb high, sleep low when you can.

Emergency steps

  • Stop climbing at the first symptoms and rest.
  • If symptoms get worse, go down at least 300 to 500 m straight away.
  • Never leave a sick person alone.
  • Give oxygen if the team has it.
  • Call local rescue or your guide. India emergency number: 112.

About this guide

SummitSafe Treks is a student project built to help new trekkers pick a suitable trail and understand the risks of altitude. Most altitude emergencies happen because people climb too fast, not because the mountain is too hard. Our goal is simple: clear information, honest warnings, and a safe return for everyone.

Built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. The trail data is approximate. Always confirm conditions with a local guide before you go.
